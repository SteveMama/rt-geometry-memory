1 — Geometry characterization

Motivation for using hidden-state geometry as a scoring signal. Conversation-state trajectories live on a unit hypersphere and are far more compact than shuffled controls, and geometric distortion tightly predicts decoder drift.

Rank-95 ≈ 1.05–1.53 (near one-dimensional) vs shuffled baseline ≈ 1.46–1.80. Geometric distortion predicts decoder drift with ρ = 0.989–0.994 across all three models. This motivates using transported geometry as a compression scoring signal.

Gate 1 passes at 17× the threshold using logit-defined harm. Semantic scoring achieves Kendall τ ≈ 0.013 (essentially flat); query-conditioned geometry achieves 0.50–0.53 — a 40× improvement in ranking ability. Query-conditioned projection matters: −76.6 vs ambient geometry (p=0.036 row).

10 — Scope conditions and failure modes

What holds up

✓Geometry-behavior divergence is real: LongLLMLingua +103–161 logit L2, −0.07–0.18 NLL vs uniform
✓sem_qcg resolves tradeoff on MSC: best L2 AND best NLL simultaneously at budget 0.50, Δ−113.7 vs semantic (p<0.001 conv)
✓Signal-swap is load-bearing on hardset: geometry beats semantic on both metrics, effect grows with model scale (Δ NLL +1.126 nats at qwen25_3b budget 0.50, p=0.0003)
✓Support-turn rescue mechanism is consistent (17 vs 7 at 1.5B, replicated at 3B)
✓All KCD policies improve NLL vs uniform on LME (p<0.05 conv level)

Scope conditions

!Architecture-dependent: Geometry works on Qwen2.5 (instruction-heavy training). Semantic wins on Llama-3.2-3B (broad text) at all budgets (p≤0.026)
!Length-dependent: Geometry effective on 20–100 turn MSC conversations. Degrades on 400–600 turn LME (sem_qcg worse than semantic at budget 0.35, p=0.011 conv)
!Regime-dependent: Geometry strongest on constraint-critical hardset. Does not replicate geometry advantage on episodic-retrieval LME
!Oracle metric-dependent: Logit-defined harm inflates geometry advantage in Gate 1. Answer-NLL-defined harm reveals geometry fails Gate 1 on LME

Central claim — supported by evidence

Conversational memory compression requires signal-conditioned turn selection. Geometry and semantics are distinct, complementary objectives. On constraint-critical memory, geometry is the right signal (recovers support turns that semantics misses). On semantic-continuity benchmarks, query-conditioned geometry inside a semantic shortlist resolves the geometry-behavior tradeoff — achieving the best geometric fidelity and behavioral quality simultaneously. Neither signal is universally dominant: architecture and conversation length both define the regime where each excels.
