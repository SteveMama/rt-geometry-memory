Mandatory sections

Every NLP paper needs these. Missing any of them risks desk rejection at a venue or confused readers on arXiv.

AbstractSelf-contained: motivation → gap → method → finding → implication. ≤250 words. ≤1920 characters for the arXiv metadata field. Write last.
IntroductionAnswer "why" before "how". State 3–4 numbered contributions explicitly. Roadmap at the end.
Related WorkTheme-grouped — NOT a list. Draw conclusions. Identify the gap your work fills. Three groups for us: prompt compression, memory benchmarks, hidden-state geometry.
Method / BackgroundKCD codec math, ternary action space, budget constraint, all three scoring signals defined formally. Self-contained. Every symbol defined on first use.
ExperimentsDatasets (MSC, LME, hardset), models (Qwen 1.5B, 3B, Llama 3B), baselines (uniform, recency, LongLLMLingua), metrics (logit L2, answer NLL), ALL hyperparameters.
ResultsTables + narrative. State significance level. Row-level vs conversation-level clearly labeled. Report ALL results including unflattering ones (scope conditions are a contribution, not a failure).
Analysis / DiscussionWhy do the results look the way they do? Mechanism (support-turn rescue). Scope conditions (architecture, length). Geometry-behavior divergence implications.
LimitationsRequired at ACL/ARR; good practice for arXiv. Must be explicit. Three paragraphs: architecture-dependence, length-dependence, oracle metric circularity.
Conclusion1–2 paragraphs. Restate main finding. No new claims.
ReferencesAll cited. Published versions preferred over preprints. Use ACL Anthology for NLP papers.
Write in this order: Method → Results → Experiments → Discussion → Related Work → Introduction → Abstract. Never write the Introduction or Abstract first — you don't know what you found yet.

What reviewers score

ARR (ACL/EMNLP) uses three axes. Soundness is the hard gate — low soundness cannot be rescued by excitement.

Soundness — what kills it
M2Reproducibility details missing (hyperparams, seeds, splits)
M4Unmotivated model or dataset selection
R1No statistical significance testing
R2Overclaimed generalizations
R5Missing significance tests
Excitement — what kills it
G1Unclear research question
G3Missing or misrepresented related work
—Contribution presented as bigger than it is
—No clear "so what" — implications not stated
Biased review heuristics reviewers are explicitly told to avoid (and that you should not worry about): "results seem obvious in retrospect", "method is too simple", "not state-of-the-art on all benchmarks", "could add experiment X". Do not pre-emptively pad the paper to block these.

Style rules

Language

Say "use" not "utilize"
Say "new" not "novel"
Lowercase: "large language models", "transformer", "attention"
Short sentences — split anything over 30 words
No vacuous words: "distinct", "significant" (without a p-value)

Formatting

Tables: \toprule / \midrule / \bottomrule
Bold best result per row
Report mean ± std or 95% CI — not just point estimates
\citep{} parenthetical · \citet{} textual
Figures: vector PDF only (no PNG/JPEG for diagrams)

Contributions and abstract

4 numbered contributions (paste into Introduction §1)

1. We demonstrate geometry-behavior divergence at scale: methods optimizing NLL can catastrophically degrade hidden-state geometric fidelity (+103–161 logit L2 units), motivating dual-objective evaluation of memory compression.

2. We introduce sem_qcg, a query-conditioned geometry signal within a semantic shortlist, and show it is the only KCD variant achieving simultaneously best geometric fidelity AND behavioral quality on MSC (Δ−113.7 L2, Δ−0.056 NLL vs semantic at budget 0.50, both p<0.001).

3. We characterize two scope conditions — architecture-dependence and length-dependence — that bound where geometry-based signals add value over semantic scoring.

4. We provide a support-turn rescue mechanism account explaining why geometry works on constraint-critical memory: user instructions produce larger hidden-state displacement than semantically similar assistant echoes (17/36 rescues vs 7/36 for semantic).

Abstract draft (238 words)

Multi-turn conversation compression must balance two distinct objectives: preserving the geometric structure of the model's hidden-state representation (geometric fidelity) and maintaining behavioral output quality. We show these objectives can diverge — LongLLMLingua, a method optimizing for answer likelihood, incurs logit L2 penalties of +103–161 units relative to uniform sampling while providing only marginal NLL improvement on MSC (1,000 conversations, 163k evaluation rows). We introduce the Keep/Compress/Drop (KCD) codec, a sparse ternary turn-selection framework, and study three scoring signals: geometry-based, semantic-based, and query-conditioned geometry within a semantic shortlist (sem_qcg). On MSC (50 conversations, Qwen2.5-1.5B), sem_qcg achieves logit L2 lower than uniform sampling at budget 0.50 — more geometrically faithful than random retention — while simultaneously improving answer NLL (both p<0.001, conversation-level signflip). On a synthetic constraint-critical stress set, geometry-based scoring outperforms semantic scoring by up to +1.1 nats NLL (p<0.001 at 3B scale), an effect that grows with model size. We identify two scope conditions: the geometry advantage is architecture-dependent (present on Qwen2.5 but reversed on Llama-3.2-3B) and length-dependent (degrades on 400–600 turn conversations). Together, these results establish geometry-behavior divergence as a real and consequential phenomenon, and demonstrate that turn scoring signal is a first-class design decision in conversational memory compression.

arXiv submission steps

1
Account and endorsement
Register at arxiv.org. First-time cs.CL submitters may need endorsement from an established author. Institutional email often auto-endorses. To request: find a recent cs.CL author, send title + abstract.
2
Choose LaTeX template
Use ACL 2025 official style files (same for arXiv preprint and conference submission). Template: acl_latex.tex from acl-org GitHub. For standalone arXiv: \documentclass[11pt]{article} + \usepackage{arxiv} also works.
3
Write the paper (see section plan above)
Writing order: Method → Results → Experiments → Discussion → Related Work → Introduction → Abstract. Get at least one external read before submitting.
4
Local compilation
Run: pdflatex main.tex → bibtex main → pdflatex main.tex → pdflatex main.tex. This generates the .bbl file. arXiv does NOT run bibtex — the .bbl must be included.
5
Package for upload
Create a .zip with: main.tex, main.bbl, acl_natbib.bst, acl.sty, all figures as PDF, appendix.tex if separate. Do NOT include: .aux, .log, compiled .pdf. Max 50MB.
6
Fill metadata on arXiv
Title (exact), Authors (name + affiliation in parens, city+country only), Abstract (≤1920 chars — check with a character counter), Primary category: cs.CL, Cross-list: cs.LG, License: CC BY 4.0.
7
Review and submit
arXiv recompiles server-side — review the generated PDF carefully. Check: figures present, fonts correct (no bitmap substitution), bibliography complete. Announcements daily at 20:00 ET Sun–Thu. Submit before 14:00 ET to appear that night.
8
After submission
Paper gets arXiv ID (e.g. 2406.XXXXX). ACL venues allow arXiv posting at any time (policy updated 2024). Revisions: submit v2 via "Replace" — all versions remain accessible.

What we can and cannot claim

Can claim strong evidence

✓Geometry-behavior divergence is real at scale (MSC 1000-conv, p<0.001)
✓sem_qcg wins both metrics on MSC simultaneously (p<0.001 conv, both objectives)
✓Geometry beats semantic on constraint-critical memory at both 1.5B and 3B
✓NLL damage from wrong signal grows with model scale (0.628 → 1.126 nats)
✓Support-turn rescue mechanism is reproducible (17/36 vs 7/36)
✓All KCD policies improve NLL vs uniform on LME (p<0.05 conv)

Cannot claim evidence is bounded

✗Geometry universally better than semantic — falsified by Llama-3.2-3B
✗sem_qcg works on very long conversations — falsified by LME at 0.35 (p=0.011)
✗Gate 1 validates geometry on answer-NLL harm — fails on LME
✗QA accuracy improves — LME QA proxy metric is invalid
Frame scope conditions as contributions. The paper contributes: (1) a dual-objective evaluation framework that catches what single-metric evaluation misses, (2) a characterization of when geometry adds value, (3) two informative negatives identifying failure modes.

Pre-submission checklist

Content

  • Every claim in Introduction has a pointer to a result
  • All hyperparameters reported (budgets, batch size, seeds)
  • Every Δ in results tables has a p-value
  • Row-level vs conversation-level clearly labeled throughout
  • Limitations section present and honest (3 paragraphs)
  • Ethics / broader impact statement included
  • Code/data availability statement included
  • All 4 contributions delivered in results

Formatting and submission

  • Bold best result per row in every table
  • All figures have self-contained captions
  • Proofread: no "novel", "utilize", no sentences >30 words
  • Abstract ≤1920 characters (paste into counter)
  • Local compile clean — no LaTeX errors or warnings
  • .bbl file included in submission zip
  • Categories set: cs.CL primary, cs.LG cross-list
  • Server-compiled PDF reviewed before final submit
